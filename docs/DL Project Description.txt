Abstract of Original Paper
The paper proposes a five-stage hybrid BiGRU + LSTM pipeline for sentiment
analysis of online product reviews: (1) web crawling, (2) NLP preprocessing

MLP baselines. Critically, no pre-trained Transformer (BERT, RoBERTa) is
tested, no cross-domain validation is performed, and the model is not evaluated

7. Tips for a Successful Submission
1. Start with reproducibility. Before attempting any improvement, reproduce
the original paper’s baseline results. If you cannot reproduce the baseline within
10% of the reported number, investigate why before proceeding. A strong paper
always validates the baseline first. It is also worth 10% of the total mark, in
addition to the 40% allocated to the project.
2. Use version control from day one. Create a GitHub repository on Day 1
and commit your code daily. This protects you if your machine fails and provides
the commit history that proves your work is original.
3. Save everything. Log every training run with timestamps, hyperparameters,
and results. You will need this for the reproducibility package and to defend your
results.
4. The ablation study is not optional. Reviewers at IF 3+ journals will reject
a paper without an ablation study. Your paper must contain at least one table
showing the incremental contribution of each component you added.
5. Write the LATEX as you go. Do not leave the writing until the last two days.
Write a paragraph after each experiment. Your methods section can be written
while you are coding.
6. Grad-CAM adds credibility. For all vision papers (Studies 1, 4, 5, N4,
N6), Grad-CAM visualisations are not optional — they are expected by medical
imaging and agricultural AI journals and transform a numerical improvement
paper into an interpretable AI paper.
7. Cross-dataset validation is the difference between a 3-point and a
4-point paper. For every study where a second public dataset is available, use
it. Testing on a single dataset is considered a major weakness at IF 4+ journals.
8. Report per-class metrics, not just overall accuracy. Overall accuracy can
be misleading in imbalanced datasets. Always report per-class F1 scores, and
highlight improvements on the minority/hardest classes.
